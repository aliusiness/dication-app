# Summarizer Types — which are most popular (research)

Date: 2026-07-12
Requested by Ali: compare popularity/demand of summarizer TYPES (book,
video/YouTube, meeting, PDF, research paper, web article), finance filter
dropped. Data only — Ali decides.
Method: web research of 2026 AI-summarizer tool roundups and reviews.

## Popularity ranking (by visible demand / tool activity)

1. YouTube / video summarizers — largest consumer demand. Tools:
   summarize.tech, NoteGPT, Notta, ScreenApp, MyMap, plus a FREE Chrome
   extension "YouTube Summary with ChatGPT & Claude."
2. Meeting / audio summarizers — largest BUSINESS demand. Tools: Otter.ai,
   Krisp, Summary AI. These record + transcribe live (a prompt can't).
3. Research-paper / academic summarizers — solid specialized demand.
   Tools: Scholarcy, Paperguide. Structured output (objectives, method,
   findings).
4. PDF / general document summarizers — broad but generic. Notion AI,
   Claude (200k context handles a whole book/contract).
5. Web-article / quick summaries — pure commodity. TLDR This, QuillBot,
   Wordtune — mostly free.

## The finding that decides prompt-viability

The summarizer market is dominated by dedicated TOOLS and browser
extensions, MANY FREE — not by prompts. A summary PROMPT sold on
PromptBase competes against free apps that do it better (they record,
handle files, run automatically). So high popularity here does NOT mean
high prompt-sellability — often the opposite (most-popular types have the
most free tools).

Most prompt-VIABLE (where a crafted prompt still adds value competitors
can't easily give away free): specialized PROFESSIONAL document
summarizers — research papers, legal contracts, financial filings — where
structure and domain expertise matter more than raw text-shortening.
Least viable: YouTube/article summaries (flooded with free tools).

## For Ali (data only)

Most POPULAR type: YouTube/video (consumer), meetings (business).
Most SELLABLE-as-a-prompt type: specialized professional document
summarizer. These two answers point in different directions — that gap is
the decision Ali makes.

## Addendum — the "sandwich": popular AND sellable

Ali's question: find a summarizer type that is BOTH popular AND sellable
as a prompt. Scored the strongest candidates on both axes.

WINNER: **Contract / legal document summarizer.**
- Popular: freelancers and small businesses sign contracts constantly
  without legal review; freelance legal market ~$4.8B; sources name an
  "identified gap in consumer/SMB contract review."
- Sellable: real willingness to pay — $10–50 per document, $19.99–99/mo
  for existing tools (far above $3.99 prompts). High stakes (missing an
  IP/non-compete/auto-renewal clause is costly) → people pay for
  confidence. Works as a prompt on pasted contract text.
- Bonus: the Honest Analyst FORMAT ports almost directly — paste document
  → plain-English summary → red-flag clauses → what to check → educational
  disclaimer. Same DNA, new domain.
- RISK: liability. Summarizing contracts edges toward legal advice → needs
  a firm "educational only, not legal advice" frame (same discipline as
  the finance "not financial advice" line). Also opens a second shelf
  (legal) apart from finance.

Runner-up: research-paper summarizer — very popular (SciSummary alone:
900k users, 2.2M papers) BUT flooded with free tools (Scholarcy, SciSpace,
QuillBot) and low student willingness-to-pay. Popular, weakly sellable.

Rejected (popular but NOT prompt-sellable): YouTube/video and meeting
summarizers — dominated by free apps/extensions a prompt can't match.

Recommendation to Ali (his decision): build product #2 as a contract/legal
document summarizer, reusing the Honest Analyst format with a legal
educational disclaimer. Data only — Ali decides.
