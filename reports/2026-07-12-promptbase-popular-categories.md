# PromptBase Market — What Actually Sells (research round)

Date: 2026-07-12
Requested by Ali. Scope: what are the most popular / best-selling prompt
categories on PromptBase, and where do summary prompts fit.
Method: web research of 2026 seller guides and PromptBase's own pages.
LIMIT: this is guide/blog-level data, not PromptBase's live bestseller
rankings (those aren't reachable with current tools). Live category
rankings are the Prompt Scout's post-launch job, watching the real site.

Per the project's hard boundary: this report GATHERS data. Ali reads it
and makes the business decision himself.

## Top-selling categories (most-cited)

1. Business / utility text prompts — marketing copy (ad copy, email
   sequences, landing pages, social posts) is the #1 text subcategory.
   Also SEO article writers, e-commerce product descriptions, coding
   assistants.
2. Midjourney / image-generation prompts — the second-largest category
   overall; "unique styles" (a specific hard-to-copy aesthetic) is
   PromptBase's own top-cited image category.
3. Meta-prompt generators — prompts that make prompts. Highest price
   band, $9.99–$14.99.
4. Claude and Gemini prompts — rapidly growing; early sellers capture
   demand before competition arrives (an early-mover opening).

## Pricing reality

- Typical single prompt: $1.99–$9.99.
- Premium (styles, mockups, portrait): $4.99–$9.99.
- Meta-prompt generators: $9.99–$14.99.
- New sellers: ~10–20 sales and $50–$200/month by month three.

## The finding that matters for Ali

- "Business-focused prompts that replace a consultant or copywriter
  offer buyers the highest perceived return on investment." A stock
  fundamental-analysis prompt is exactly this: it replaces (part of) an
  analyst. Ali's Honest Analyst sits INSIDE the strongest-selling,
  highest-ROI category.
- Summary prompts: NOT cited as a popular or dedicated selling category
  anywhere in the research. No evidence summaries are a top seller. The
  likely reason (consistent with earlier rounds): every AI summarizes
  for free, so standalone summaries are cheap and crowded.
- Where summaries DO have value: specialized, inside a domain (e.g. a
  finance report summarizer) — which keeps the seller on one shelf.

## What the data does NOT say

It does not say "sell summaries." It does not rank a specific top-3 with
live numbers (not reachable now). Ali decides the niche; this is data only.

## Addendum — broad research beyond PromptBase (how people use AI overall)

Washington Post-cited breakdown of generative-AI uses: creative writing /
role-play 21%, homework help 18%, search 17%, work/business tasks 15%,
coding 7%, image generation 6%, health advice 5%, jailbreaks 4%.

ChatGPT's most common tasks: research, content/writing (incl. summarizing),
coding (single most common task = debugging, ~6% of all chats),
business/productivity. Notably: "teams in finance, marketing and operations
use it to summarize findings and turn raw numbers into actionable insights."

KEY DISTINCTION (the real lesson): summarization IS a very common USE — people
do it constantly. But common ≠ sellable. It's common precisely because it's
easy to do yourself for free, which is why standalone summary prompts have low
willingness-to-pay. What sells = common-NEED but HARD-to-do-well
(consultant-replacement, specialized styles, structured finance analysis).

Finance fits the sellable side: millions ask AI about money (master file §3),
AND doing it well is hard (needs structure, discipline, no invented numbers) —
so "turn raw financial numbers into honest insight" is both in-demand and
defensible. That is exactly what the Honest Analyst already does.
